Financial Planner

For each year...
  1. Calculate gains to accounts per ROI values entered, and add to running totals for ordinary income, or capital gains.
  2. Withdraw money from IRA for RMD, or Roth conversion, as needed. Add that value to taxable income. Add to Roth if we are doing conversions.
  3. If we get Social Security, add 85% of it to the running taxable income total. Starting in 2032 SS is reduced to 87% of what we should get.
  4. Medicare is calculated from taxable income from two years prior, or the data entered on the form for the first two yeaers.
  5. Calculate income tax, where taxable income is 85% of Social Security + IRA distribution + income from savings and CD + 50% of mutual funds income. Note that 50% of mutual funds income is considered capital gains. The 50/50 arrangement is an estimate.
  6. Income taxes are paid for from IRA distributions or mutual funds, stocks, and CD, depending on the "Pay Tax from IRA" checkbox. If paid from mutual funds, stocks, and CD, then the deductions are equivilant to their relative sizes. The amount withdrawn is increased by 20%, an estimate, to pay for the additional capital gains tax we incure due to this withdrawl. If there is not enough money in mutual funds, stocks, and CD, then we take money from Roth.
  7. Calculate the actual capital gains tax per the withdrawls made above.
  8. If there is more than enough cash to pay taxes, the overage is either added to the Roth, or the CD. If there is less, then the deficit is deducted from the CD. The over/under is displayed in the "Excess" column. A positive number is an excess.
  9. The yearly spending target is increased by the inflation amount, and Social Security is increased by 99% of the inflation amount.
The Form
  • Model Name - Any name you want. This will become the file name if you save the data from the form.
  • Yearly Spending Target - The amount of money you expect to spend each year, other than taxes or medicare premiums, as they are accounted for by the model elsewhere. It becomes part of the "Expense" column in the report. It is increased by the inflation amount each year.
  • Inflation Rate - Expected rate of inflation. It affects Yearly Spending Target and Social Security amount.
  • Current Age - Age now. The first year of the model. It affects the Required Mandatory Distribution from your IRA.
  • Year - The current year. It affects Social Security, as that is expected to be reduced in 2032.
  • End Simulation at Age - The simulation will end at this age.
  • SS Amount - Monthly Social Security amount. The value is increased each year by the Inflation Rate.
  • Begin SS at Age / Month - The year and month you expect to start Social Security.
  • Income Last Year / Year Before - Adjusted gross income from the last two years. These numbers are used to calculate Medicare premiums the first two years of the model. After that the numbers generated by the model are used.
  • Savings / ROI - Amount of current savings and the interest rate. Not currently used by the model.
  • CD / ROI - Amount of any CDs and interest rate. These funds are used, together with Mutual Funds and Stocks, to pay taxes. In any given year, if the model algorithm takes too much out of various accounts to cover expenses, then the money is returned to this account. Proceeds from this account are taxed as ordinary income.
  • Mutual Fund / ROI - Amount of any mutual funds and expected rate of return. These funds are used, together with CD and stocks, to pay taxes. Note that returns from mutual funds can be either ordinary income, from interest and dividends, or capital gains, from the sale of stock. The model makes a 50/50 percent assumption and pays 50% of gains as ordinary income, and the remaining 50% as capital gains. But, when funds are extracted to pay taxes, they are taxed as capital gains.
  • Stocks / ROI - Amount of any stocks and expected rate of return. These funds, together with CD and Mutual Funds are used to pay taxes. Proceeds from this account are taxed as capital gains.
  • IRA / ROI - Amount of any IRAs and expected rate of return. These funds can be converted to Roth over time, depending on inputs to the model (see below). They are also withdrawn based on the rules for RMDs begining at age 73. Proceeds from this account are taxed as ordinary income.
  • Roth / ROI - Amount of any Roth and expected rate of return. Funds in the IRA can optionally be moved to this account, over time, depending on inputs to the model (see below). Proceeds from this account are not taxed. Money from this account is automatically withdrawn to pay taxes if the model runs out of money in the CD, Mutual Funds, and Stocks accounts. The model does not enforce the 5 year wait rule.
  • Roth Conversion Amount / Years - The amount you want to convert from IRA to Roth and the number of years to repeat this event. Optionally if there is an amount, but the years is left blank, then the model will repeat the conversion, using the amount, for as long as there is still money in the IRA. Or, if there is no amount, but the number of years is entered, then the model will calculate how much money it should convert each year to complete the conversion in the requisit number of years.
  • Pay Tax from IRA - Check this box and the simulation will attempt to pay income tax from the IRA distribution, whether it be mandatory or a conversion. This potentially reduces the Roth conversion amount, so increase the amount to compensate, or not. If there is not enough IRA money to pay all expenses, then money is deducted from the CD, first, and then the Roth.
Buttons
  • Show/Hide Help - Alternately displays this help or hides it.
  • Retirieve Data From File - Displays a search box so you can find and select a previously saved file to populate the form.
  • Simulation - Run a simulation based on data entered to the form.
  • Save Data - Saves data from the form to a file using the Model Name as the name of the file. Only data entered to the form is saved. The simulation/report is not saved.
Report Columns
  • Age - Subjects age for current year.
  • Total Assets - Sum of all accounts at end of year after interest earned and expenses paid, divided by 1000.
  • Mutual Funds - Balance at end of year for mutual fund account.
  • Stocks - Balance at end of year for stocks.
  • CD - Balance at end of year for CD account.
  • IRA - Balance at end of year for the IRA.
  • Roth - Balance at end of year for Roth account.
  • IRA Dist - Total distributions for RMA and Roth conversions.
  • Roth Dist - Total distributions from Roth account. Only used when there are not enough funds in mutual funds, stocks, and CD to pay taxes.
  • Taxable Income - 85% of Social Security, plus IRA distribution, plus savings interest, plus CD interest, plus 50% income from mutual funds.
  • Cap Gains - Gains from the sale of stocks plus 50% of income from mutual funds.
  • Income Tax - Tax on ordinary income.
  • Cap Gains Tax - Tax on capital gains.
  • Medicare - Medicare premiums based on income.
  • Expenses - Sum of spending target, income tax, capital gains tax, and medicare premiumns. The spending target is increased each year by the inflation rate.
  • Cash - Sum of Social Security and withdrawls from all accounts, except IRA distributions that are converted to Roth.
  • Excess - The difference between cash and expenses after all withdrawls, and taxes and medicare premiums are paid. A positive number indicates a excess; a negative number indicates a shortage. An excess is added to the Roth, if applicable, or the CD. A shortage is withdrawn from the CD, first, and then the Roth.
  • Note - Notes (see below).
  • Other - Used for debugging.
Notes
  1. Take money from the IRA to pay a shortage.
  2. Take money from Roth to pay for a shortage.
  3. Take money from stock, mutual fund, and cd to pay a shortage, and those accounts are exhausted.
  4. Take money from stock, mutual fund, and cd to pay a shortage.
  5. Make a Roth contribution.
  6. Deposit excess to CD.
  7. Take money from the CD to pay the shortage.
  8. Take money from the CD to pay the shortage and the CD is exhausted.
